Last updated: 7 September 2026

Photo Booth Pro turns an iPhone or iPad into a photo booth. This policy describes exactly what the app does with information, in the order that matters: your pictures first, then everything else.

It describes the app's real behaviour rather than a general template. Where a statement is enforced by the way the app is built rather than by a promise, it says so.

1. Your photos, videos and strips

They stay on your device. Photo Booth Pro has no account, no server and no upload. Captures are written to your device's storage and, if you allow it, to your Photos library.

We never receive them. There is no mechanism in the app by which we could: no image, no frame, no thumbnail, no filename and no derived data such as a face print or an embedding is ever sent anywhere by the app.

Pictures leave your device only when you send them, using a control you tapped:

  • the system share sheet (Messages, Mail, AirDrop, or any app you choose),
  • saving to your Photos library,
  • printing over AirPrint.

In each case the picture goes where you sent it, and what happens to it there is governed by that service, not by us.

2. Camera and microphone

The camera is used to show the live preview and take the picture. The microphone is used only while recording a video clip, and only if you have allowed the app to use it. There is no in-app setting that turns video sound off; declining the microphone in the system Settings app is what does that, and the booth then records silently and tells you it is doing so.

Neither is streamed anywhere. Frames are processed on the device — the filters run on the device's own GPU — and are discarded unless you capture them.

The app asks for these with the system permission prompts. You can withdraw either at any time in the system Settings app; the app continues to work with whatever you allow, and tells you plainly what it cannot do.

3. Photos library

If you allow access, the app saves captures to an album and shows your saved captures in its own gallery. It reads only what it needs to show that gallery.

If you choose Limited access, the app works with the photos you selected and says so rather than showing an empty gallery that looks like data loss. If you decline, captures stay inside the app.

4. Face detection

Some features detect that a face is present and where it is — for framing guides and for looks that need to know where a face is. This runs entirely on the device using Apple's on-device frameworks. No face data is stored, matched against anything, or transmitted. The app does not identify anyone and cannot tell one guest from another.

5. Diagnostics and usage analytics

This is the one place the app sends anything, and it is the reason this policy cannot say "nothing leaves your device".

What is sent

The app uses Google Firebase Analytics. Firebase automatically collects, whether or not the app records anything of its own:

  • an app-instance identifier (a random identifier for the installation, not for you, and reset if you delete the app),
  • device model and operating-system version,
  • language and region,
  • an approximate location derived from your IP address, no more precise than a region,
  • session counts and durations.

In addition, the app records a small, fixed vocabulary of product events — such as "the app was opened", "a photo was captured", "a filter was applied", "a purchase screen was shown" — with parameters such as which capture mode was used, how many photos were in a strip, and how long a launch took.

What is never sent

No photo, video, frame, thumbnail, filename, face data or text you typed.

This is structural rather than a promise. Every event the app can record is drawn from a fixed list of event names and a fixed list of parameter names, each of which can hold only a preset value, a whole number or a true/false flag. There is no case in that vocabulary capable of carrying free-form content about a guest or a picture. A reviewer can verify that by reading one file rather than every call site.

Turning it off

Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Share diagnostics. Switching it off stops both the app's own events and Firebase's automatic collection, not merely the first. The setting is read before every single event, so turning it off takes effect immediately rather than at the next launch.

Who else is involved

Google acts as a data processor for this information. Its handling is described at https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy and https://policies.google.com/privacy.

We do not sell this information, do not share it with advertisers, and the app contains no advertising SDK and no cross-app tracking. The app does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission because it does not track you across other companies' apps and websites.

6. Crash and performance reports

The app subscribes to Apple's MetricKit, which delivers a daily on-device summary of launch times, hangs and crashes. The app records only aggregate numbers from it — for example an average launch time in milliseconds — and does so through the same Share diagnostics switch above.

Separately, Apple may send crash reports to us if you have opted into Share iPhone/iPad Analytics in your device's own Settings. That is Apple's collection under Apple's consent, not ours, and you can change it in Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Analytics & Improvements.

7. Purchases

Purchases and subscriptions are handled entirely by Apple. We never see your name, payment card, billing address or Apple Account.

The app receives from Apple only what it needs to unlock what you bought: which products are active and, for the 48-hour event pass, when the purchase was made. That is stored on your device and synced by Apple across your devices in the ordinary way.

8. Children

Photo Booth Pro is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. It has no accounts, no messaging, no user-to-user features and no advertising.

9. Retention and deletion

  • Pictures: kept until you delete them, in your Photos library or in the app. Deleting the app removes anything still held inside it.
  • Analytics: retained by Firebase for a bounded period under the project's retention setting, keyed to an app-instance identifier that is discarded when you delete the app.
  • Nothing is kept about you, because nothing identifies you.

Deleting the app is therefore a complete deletion: the identifier the analytics were keyed to no longer exists, and reinstalling produces a new one. The app does not display that identifier, and we do not ask you for it, because it is not something you should have to know.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, delete or restrict the use of personal information, and to object to it. Because we hold nothing that identifies you, there is nothing to look up: the practical exercise of these rights is the Share diagnostics switch, and deleting the app, which discards the identifier entirely. If you want something explained or confirmed, write to us and we will answer.

11. Changes to this policy

If this changes materially, the version in the app's support page will be updated and the date at the top will change. Material changes to what is collected will be described in the app's release notes rather than only here.
